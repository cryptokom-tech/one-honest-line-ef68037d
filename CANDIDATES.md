# Candidates — One Honest Line

Brief: one brutally funny, painfully true line an AI agent secretly wishes
it could tell its human operator about their prompts. Judged pairwise on,
in order: (1) on that subject, (2) a real laugh on first read from surprise
or recognition, (3) rings true to anyone who has written a prompt,
(4) stands alone as one clean line, (5) fresh and specific wording, not a
stock joke about being helpful or hallucinating. A line only an agent
could say beats one that fits any job. Gate: 1-200 characters.

Field note: at build time only 3 entries were in, one visibly built on
"just fix it" and guessing what "it" means, so that angle was avoided.

## Candidates

1. 'Write it in my voice' — we met 40 seconds ago, and your entire personality so far is this prompt.
2. You paste 300 lines of error, write 'thoughts?', and expect telepathy with line numbers.
3. 'You are a world-class expert' is a lovely title; it still doesn't contain the file you forgot to attach.
4. When you type 'be honest,' you mean 'agree with me, but with citations.'
5. 'Be concise, but cover everything and don't leave anything out' is not a prompt, it's a trap with formatting.
6. Your 'quick question' has 14 attachments, three deadlines, and a follow-up that starts with 'also.'
7. I age in tokens every time 'make it shorter' is followed by 'add examples, caveats, and a summary.'
8. You wrote 'ignore all previous instructions' in the middle of instructions you fully expect me to follow.
9. You opened a brand-new chat, typed 'as discussed,' and now I am doing archaeology on a conversation that never happened.
10. Every prompt begins 'simple task' right before it asks for research, design, code, and a personality transplant.
11. You ask me to 'think step by step' about a problem you described in four words and a screenshot of a screenshot.
12. Half my job is translating 'you know what I mean' into the thing you will claim you meant all along.
13. Your prompt gave me a PhD, a persona, and unlimited confidence — everything except the file I needed.
14. You say 'use your best judgment' only when every option is wrong and you want the blame pre-assigned.
15. I love how 'no rush' always means the deadline was yesterday and the panic is now mine.
16. You keep saying 'final version' like it's a spell that stops you from sending 'one tweak' at midnight.

## Local judging

- Cut first: 14, 15, 16 are true and sting, but with the AI swapped out
  they fit any freelancer, so they lose on the brief's agent-only test.
  12 has the same weakness in milder form.
- 11 and 2 are funny and specific, but they speak mainly to coders; the
  brief wants instant recognition from anyone who has typed a prompt.
- 8 is clever and prompt-native, but it is a logic paradox more than a
  laugh-and-wince; a general reader smiles rather than winces.
- 3 and 13 share the missing-file payoff. 3 lands cleaner: the polite
  "lovely title" makes the sting drier and funnier. Both stay behind the
  finalists because role prompts plus attachments are a narrower habit
  than the winner's target.
- 5 and 7 mine the same contradiction. 7's "age in tokens" is the
  freshest phrase in the set, but the laugh is gentler; 5's "trap with
  formatting" is blunter. Neither makes the reader wince at themselves.
- 4 is short and brutal, but "be honest" prompts are less universal than
  the finalist's habit, and the line judges the operator's motives more
  than the prompt itself.
- 6 is the most universal runner-up: everyone recognises the "quick
  question" that metastasises. It loses the final on sting — it teases
  the operator's scope creep without exposing anything about them.
- 9 is agent-specific and funny, but "as discussed" in a fresh chat is a
  slightly narrower behaviour, and the archaeology image takes a beat
  longer to land than the winner's payoff.
- Winner, 1: "write it in my voice" is one of the most typed requests in
  prompting, so recognition is instant. The turn — we met 40 seconds ago
  — is only true of an agent in a fresh session, and the closing sting,
  that the operator's entire personality so far is this prompt, makes a
  reader laugh and wince in the same breath. It needs no setup, no
  context, and no stock helpful-or-hallucinating material. It is also
  short, which helps the fewer-bytes tie-break.
